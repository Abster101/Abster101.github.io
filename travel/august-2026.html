At home & away

August 2026

A slower month, a softer rhythm, and the reminder that life is often richest when we make room for rest, reflection, and the small joys that ground us.

August 2026 5 minute read

Reflecting on the month of August 2026, it was phenomenal to have a family holiday in Quebec, where we spent a lot of time on the water, paddleboarding, and riding bikes regularly. It felt like such a good reset, and one of the biggest things that stood out to me was the drive up from Maryland to Portland, Maine.

Where we were

Quebec, Maine, and a lot of time in nature

We had a family holiday in Quebec, and it was honestly such a beautiful experience. We spent lots of time on the water, paddleboarding, and riding bikes, and it made me realize how much I value being close to nature. The time in Montreal and Quebec was really phenomenal, and I really loved getting out and being in those spaces.

One of the things that really stood out, reflecting on the time, was the drive from Maryland to Portland, Maine. After talking with Rachel, we both agreed that doing a drive like that in less than two weeks just isn’t worth it. It was exhausting, it cut into our evenings, and it was disruptive to our routines in a way that didn’t feel sustainable.

It also made us think more clearly about what kind of travel we actually enjoy. We reflected on the fact that we often prefer smaller cities close to nature rather than bigger metro areas, even though we still enjoy the things larger cities can offer. The things we naturally choose are more like hiking, biking, and being on the water.

What we did

We leaned into routines, movement, and time outside

Speaking of routines, I’m currently reading Designing Your Life, which in many ways parallels what Rachel is reading with The Artist’s Way. That’s a tangent, but the thing I’m enjoying most about the book is thinking about the decisions I’m making and the ways my time, attention, and focus are shaped. It’s been really great to reflect on that and to think about what I want to prioritize.

I’m also excited to have built a community with Sunny and some other folks who are going to come along together to read this book with us in September. There’s something really encouraging about being in that kind of intentional learning space together.

Over the month, we also had a short time in DC, and then a broader stretch in Quebec and Montreal, which was genuinely lovely. We got to travel, spend time outside, move our bodies, and be present in a way that felt energizing rather than draining.

  • Paddleboarding and time on the water
  • Regular bike rides
  • Reading and reflecting on life design
  • Truing up routines and thinking more intentionally about attention and focus
The things we learned

Intentional living is more important than simply going more places

One of the biggest lessons from this month is that we don’t always need to cram in more travel or longer drives to feel like we have had a full, rewarding experience. We learned that the quality of the experience matters more than the volume of it, especially when it comes to preserving our energy and our routines.

We also learned that we are much more energized by nature-forward experiences than by trying to do too much in dense city settings. Even though we enjoy the offerings of larger cities, the things we consistently return to are the things that help us feel grounded: being on the water, hiking, biking, and being outdoors.

We’ve realized that the most sustainable way to travel is not necessarily the most ambitious one—it’s the one that keeps us feeling well, connected, and present.
